FLS Calculation Methods

pyCaRM precomputes a thermal response matrix using the Finite Line Source (FLS) analytical model, with its real part and, by default, its image part (see Real and image parts). Two methods are available to evaluate the effective distance between boreholes, controlled by the fls_mode parameter of Simulation.

Real and image parts

The FLS response combines a real part and, by default, an image part, set by the fls_image parameter of Simulation (True by default, False for the real part only). The real part is the borehole as a line source of length \(H = L\) in an infinite medium. The image part is a sink mirrored above the ground surface: it keeps the FLS temperature perturbation at zero on the surface. The top of the borehole is buried at depth \(D = L_{sup}\), the thickness of the upper ground layer.

With \(\mathrm{ierf}(x) = x\,\mathrm{erf}(x) - (1 - e^{-x^2})/\sqrt{\pi}\), the response at distance \(d\) and time \(t\) is

\[h(d, t) = \int_{1/\sqrt{4\alpha t}}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-d^2 s^2}}{2 H s^2} \Big[\underbrace{2\,\mathrm{ierf}(Hs)}_{\text{real}} + \underbrace{2\,\mathrm{ierf}((2D + H)s) - \mathrm{ierf}(2Ds) - \mathrm{ierf}((2D + 2H)s)}_{\text{image}}\Big]\, ds\]

following Claesson and Javed (2011). This equation is validated against pygfunction (Cimmino, 2018) in the test suite.

Limitations

The FLS treats each borehole as a line source. Two cases are therefore an approximation:

  • Very short times. At the first time steps the heat has not yet travelled far from the borehole, and the line-source assumption does not describe this phase accurately.

  • Helical heat exchangers. The heat source is a helix, not a line, so the FLS interference between helical boreholes is an approximation.

Overview

Each borehole is assigned a Voronoi cell of area \(A_i\), from which an equivalent radius is derived:

\[r_{eq,i} = \sqrt{\frac{A_i}{\pi}}\]

This radius represents the effective influence area of borehole \(i\) and is used to compute the distance entering the FLS integral. Two strategies are implemented.

sqrt method (default)

The effective distance between source borehole \(j\) and receiver borehole \(i\) is computed as:

\[d_{ij} = \sqrt{d_{Euclidean,ij}^2 + r_{eq,i}^2}\]

where \(r_{eq,i}\) is the equivalent radius of the receiver cell. This is a scalar approximation — a single distance value per borehole pair — and is the default method due to its low computational cost.

continuous method

The receiver borehole wall is discretized into \(N_\theta\) equally spaced points along its equivalent radius:

\[\mathbf{p}_k = (x_i + r_{eq,i}\cos\theta_k,\ y_i + r_{eq,i}\sin\theta_k), \quad \theta_k = \frac{2\pi k}{N_\theta},\ k = 0, \ldots, N_\theta - 1\]

The FLS response is evaluated at each point and averaged:

\[\bar{h}_{ij} = \frac{1}{N_\theta} \sum_{k=0}^{N_\theta - 1} h(d_{ijk})\]

where \(d_{ijk}\) is the distance from source \(j\) to point \(k\) on receiver \(i\). This method is more accurate but significantly more expensive.

Comparing the two methods

Both methods look at the same thing: the boundary of the receiver’s cell, a circle of radius \(r_{eq,i}\). The mean squared distance from source \(j\) to the points of that circle is \(d^2 + r_{eq,i}^2\), which is exactly the squared distance to the point at 90° from the source direction. So "sqrt" evaluates the FLS once, at that point, while "continuous" averages it over \(N_\theta\) points of the circle. The two differ only because \(h\) is not linear in the distance.

Choosing a method

fls_mode

Cost

"sqrt"

Low — single distance per pair

"continuous"

High — scales with \(N_\theta \times n_{BHE}^2 \times n_{steps}\)

Use "sqrt" (default) for parametric studies and large fields. Use "continuous" when high accuracy is required and the field is small.

Load superposition

Both methods store only the response increments \(\Delta R_k = R_{k+1} - R_k\) (the FLS integral over each time step), computed once when the simulation is built. At step \(n\) the wall temperature penalty of borehole \(j\) is the superposition of the whole heat history,

\[\Delta T_j(n) = \frac{1}{2\pi k L} \sum_{t=0}^{n-1} \sum_i q_i(t)\, \Delta R_{n-1-t,\, ij}\]

evaluated as one matrix-vector product per step.

Response cache

The increments can be stored on disk with the fls_cache_dir parameter of Simulation:

simulation = Simulation(..., fls_cache_dir="fls_cache")

The increments are saved in that folder as a .npy file, with a .json file listing the inputs they were computed from (fls_mode, fls_image, time step, ground properties, borehole length and depth, field layout). A later simulation of the same field pointing at the same folder reads them instead of computing them again; flows, temperatures and weather can differ. A file from a longer run also serves a shorter one. The time step must be constant. None (default) keeps the increments in memory and writes nothing.
